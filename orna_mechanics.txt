# mechanics work, read by knowledge_search (see orna_mechanics.py). Built from
# a community beginner guide (ornalegends.com) and fact-checked against current
# (early 2026) sources: playorna.com's own codex + blog, the Orna wiki,
# playerecho.com and reddit r/OrnaRPG. This is well-informed community/player
# knowledge, similar trust to a good wiki - NOT official developer text, and
# Orna is patched roughly monthly, so treat specific numbers as "current as of
# early 2026" and prefer the live codex / patch notes for an exact figure that
# matters. (A coding-oriented twin of this, with pointers to the repo's own

=== Tiers and character level ===
Orna has 10 class tiers, not 11. The character level cap is 250; tier 10
unlocks at level 225. Players say "tier 11" for the level 225-250 stretch, but
no new class lives there - the developers added the Ascension system instead of
an 11th tier. Nothing in the codex carries a tier above 10, in any category
(verified directly: items, monsters, bosses, raids, followers, classes and
spells all top out at 10). But "★11" IS a real distinction for CONTENT rather
than classes, and it is answerable: ★11 dungeons and towers place Arisen
Superbosses on floor 16 and floor 25 (★10 puts its 5 superbosses on the final
floor only), and a character at ★11 / level 250 gets double the Godforging
chances per run versus ★10. So "there is no tier 11" is right about classes and
wrong as a flat answer to "should I run T11 dungeons". A character is three nested things, and calling any two of them by one word is
the mistake to avoid. A CLASS LINE - Mage, Thief, Warrior, Valhallan, Summoner
or Demigod, six of them - is what you belong to for life, and it holds a CLASS
at every tier from 1 to 10; a line is NOT a single class, and its tier-10 class
is only its last rung, so "the Mage line" does not mean "Heretic". The three
starting classes are Warrior, Mage and Thief (tier 1); the tree branches as you
tier up. The six tier-10 CLASSES - not "specializations" - are Gilgamesh,
Heretic, Realmshifter, Beowulf, Grand Summoner and Deity, each with Celestial
evolved variants unlocked via the Titans (e.g. Beowulf Auriga/Hydrus, Heretic
Ara/Corvus, Gilgamesh Hercules/Ursa, Deity Ara/Ursa), 18 in all. A
SPECIALIZATION is the separate passive package a class picks on top - Ranger,
Berserker, Sequencer, Duelist, Hunter, Guardian, Scholar, Stargazer, Cleric and
so on, the first unlocking around level 50. Community guides and aussiescodex
frequently call the tier-10 classes "specializations"; that wording is wrong and
reading it back produced a live wrong answer on 2026-09-27. Which LINE a given
class belongs to is not recorded in any structured source - a codex class record
has its tier and no line, and a class description's "can wield equipment of the
thief" is cross-line EQUIPMENT ACCESS rather than membership (Heretic Corvus is
a Mage-line class described as "thief").

=== Ascension Level (AL) ===
Ascension is the endgame progression PAST level 250, added around 2022 (a
community beginner guide from 2020 will not mention it). You unlock it at tier
10 (level 225) by building the Altar of Ascension in your Origin Town (about
50,000,000 orns + 1,000 stone). Each Ascension Level adds +1% to that class's
base stats, so AL 100 roughly doubles them. It is PER CLASS (all variants of
one class ascend together), not account-wide, and it does NOT raise the level
cap - it is a separate, effectively uncapped axis you push independently of
hitting level 250. AL multiplies base (class/specialization) stats; gear stats
add on top.

=== Core stats ===
The main stats are HP, Mana, Attack, Defense, Magic, Resistance, Dexterity,
Ward, Crit and Foresight, plus View Distance. There is also Crit Damage (how
much extra a crit does - a separate stat from Crit/Crit chance), Follower Stats,
Summon Stats, and %-bonus stats (orn/exp/gold/luck bonus). Ward matters more the
deeper into endgame you go (its pool is roughly (HP+Mana)/2 scaled by gear
Ward%), because late bosses hit for far more than a raw HP pool. In PVP your HP
is doubled.

=== Item quality ===
Quality is a percentage roll that multiplies an item's base stats. The tiers
(community/wiki ranges): Broken (about 70-89%), Poor (90-99%), Common/Regular
(100%), Superior (110-119%), Famed (120-130%), Legendary (140-170%), Ornate
(170-200%); 170% is a Legendary/Ornate crossover. Higher quality = higher
stats and more adornment slots.

A 2026-09-26 research document independently gives the quality bands as Ornate
+70..+100%, Legendary +40..+69%, Famed +20..+30%, Superior +10..+19%, Regular
+0%, Poor -1..-25%, Broken -26..-50%. Converted to the absolute percentages the
bot uses, that agrees with the COMMUNITY GUIDES (Superior 110-119, Famed 120-130)
and NOT with orna_assess.get_quality_code's ported boundaries (Superior 101-119,
Famed 120-139). That is now two independent sources against the port. The repo's
version stays authoritative for code; settling it needs a live playorna assess
page, which is the check CLAUDE.md already prescribes for this exact boundary.

=== Upgrading and forging ===
Items upgrade through levels: 1-10 at the Blacksmith (costs time, gold and
materials, increasing per level). Beyond 10 the upgrades are instant but
expensive: Masterforge = level 11 (at the Alchemist), Demonforge = level 12 (at
the Demonologist), and Godforge = level 13, which is NOT bought - it is a random
chance dropped by an "Arisen" superboss (yellow aura) while a demonforged item
is equipped. Material cost scales with quality (roughly quality-fraction × 333
for Masterforge, × 666 for Demonforge). Dismantling an item gives 1 material
regardless of its quality or level.

=== Adornments (jewels) ===
Adornments are gems socketed into gear for on-hit or passive effects (e.g. a
lifesteal-on-hit ruby, ward-power gems, status-protection gems). The number of
slots an item has grows with its FORGE LEVEL, not raw quality: godforged adds
+4 slots, master/demonforged +3, Ornate quality +2, any quality over 100% +1.
So masterforging an item gives it its Ornate slot count plus one. Base slot
counts run from 1 up to 12 (only weapons reach the high end), so a fully
godforged item can reach about 16 slots. Celestial weapons use their own fixed
slot progression instead. Adornments themselves are items you can look up in the
codex (they carry their own tier, rarity and stats).

=== Celestial and Arisen gear ===
Celestial gear is the top item rarity (Tier 10), crafted from refined Skyshards
and customised with Celestial Augments at the Titan Workshop - these come from
the Titans/Towers of Olympia and are endgame. "Arisen" is NOT a rarity: it is a
SOURCE tag for gear dropped by Arisen superbosses, and an Arisen item can be an
ordinary rarity like Famed. So do not treat "Arisen" as a quality level.

=== Combat: the universal damage formula ===
Every offensive interaction resolves through one equation (guild-confirmed
2026-09-26):
    Damage = ((Stat_off × M1) − (Stat_def × Buff_def) / 2) × M2 × N_strikes × Buff_off × Faction
Stat_off is Attack against the target's Defense for physical skills, or Magic
against Resistance for magical ones. M1 is the skill's PENETRATION multiplier and
M2 its raw damage multiplier (M2 usually rolls within a per-skill min/max range).
A basic attack has M1 = M2 = 1.
This is a THRESHOLD system, not a subtraction: if the bracketed part resolves to
zero or below, total damage is exactly ZERO no matter how large M2, the offensive
buffs or an elemental weakness are. That is why a high-M1 skill is mandatory
against heavily-defended endgame targets, and why multi-strike skills with large
cumulative M2 are better against low-defense targets.
Buffs are ASYMMETRIC, which follows from where they sit in the equation:
defensive buffs/debuffs change the target's defensive stat BEFORE the threshold,
while offensive buffs multiply only AFTER it. So buffing Attack or Magic on a
skill that cannot already penetrate adds nothing - any multiplier on zero is
zero. Single-tier and double-tier buffs stack with each other, and temporary
buffs add another independent layer.
Multi-strike skills roll M2 independently per strike, but the chance to apply a
status/debuff is rolled ONCE, on the first successful hit - a miss forfeits the
debuff entirely, which is what makes evasion valuable against multi-hit enemies.
The basic attack is the one exception to the buff rule: offensive buffs raise its
M1 (penetration) rather than multiplying after the threshold, and crit buffs
stack additively on it.
Hybrid skills use 65% of Attack PLUS 65% of Magic as the combined offensive stat,
against the mean of the target's Defense and Resistance - so they are worth
recommending against an enemy with a lopsided defensive profile. (orna_echo's
guide tables state the same: "Natural Hybrid | (ATT × 0.65) + (MAG × 0.65)".)

=== Dual wielding ===
Two one-handed weapons contribute 65% of their COMBINED stats - guild-stated and
then explicitly reconfirmed 2026-09-26. This is `_DUAL_WIELD_FACTOR` in
telegram_orna and is pinned by its self-check. Despite the penalty it can still
beat a two-hander: measured on a real request, a legal one-handed pair totalled
13,743 magic against the Celestial Archistaff's 12,445.
Only ONE celestial weapon may be equipped, one- or two-handed, so a "best in
every slot" pick can never be two celestials.
NOTE a 2026-09-26 research document asserted 45% for main stats and 50% for
passive world bonuses (exp/orn multipliers) instead, with a citation. The guild
rejected that figure. Recorded because it also means that document's cited
numbers are not automatically reliable - treat its prose as claims to check, not
as data.

=== Survivability: Second Chance, DoT and Steadfast ===
"Second Chance" (and class variants such as the Heretic's "Mana Rush") gives a
high chance to survive an otherwise lethal hit at exactly 1 HP.
Damage-over-time BYPASSES it completely: Bleed, Burn, Poison, Blight and Rot
resolve at the END of the turn, and lethal DoT damage kills through Second
Chance. That is what makes "Steadfast"/"Steadfast II" (which lower the chance of
a status landing at all) the real defence against DoT execution rather than a
nice-to-have. Some classes push the other way - the Assassin's "Annwn
Subterfuge" raises both the chance of inflicting a status and its duration.

=== Demonworking Tools (Anguish) ===
Demonworking Tools raise an item's Anguish Level by exactly 1, boosting its stats
for PvE Anguish content only (worth nothing in PvP). They apply only to a
Masterforged, Demonforged or Godforged item, and applying one RESETS the item to
level 10, stripping that forge status - so each use costs a full re-forge. The
material cost of re-Masterforging/Demonforging an Anguished item is permanently
reduced on later cycles, which is the only thing that makes the loop affordable.

=== Tower Shards and Celestial forging costs ===
Tower climbs yield Tower Shards, converted to Sky Shards at 10:1. Starting a
level-1 Celestial Weapon costs 5,000 Sky Shards (= 50,000 Tower Shards), and
taking one to its maximum level 20 costs a cumulative 148,800 Tower Shards. The
20-level ceiling matches orna_assess.CELESTIAL_WEAPON_SLOTS, which has exactly 20
entries. Titan Augments then redistribute a celestial weapon's stats - e.g.
socketing Ward augments onto a Celestial Axe to inject Ward into a Gilgamesh
build - so a celestial's printed stat line is not its ceiling.

=== Elements and factions ===
There are four factions, each tied to an element: Earthen Legion (Earth),
Stormforce (Lightning), Knights of Inferno (Fire), and Frozenguard (Water). You
pick one at level 10; changing faction is a small real-money purchase (about
$0.99) and is repeatable. Your faction gives +25% damage dealt with its element
and -20% damage taken from that element. Sigils (formerly "runes") mark an enemy
so a matching-element hit does bonus damage. Weapon element affects Attack
skills; an elemental spell's own element overrides the weapon's. Some
bosses/monsters are immune to, or resist, specific elements - this per-boss
resistance data is NOT in the codex; it comes from community "Monster Data" and
is the key to "how do I beat X" fights.

=== Wild Towers of Olympia ===
Five real-time endgame towers, one per Titan: Selene, Eos, Oceanus, Themis and
Prometheus (tier 9+). The wild towers auto-climb over time, starting at floor
15/16 and gaining about six floors a day across six fixed intraday checkpoints,
with 50 meaning "cleared, waiting for the reset". All five share ONE 35-day
cycle, anchored at 2023-12-07 00:00 UTC, and are offset from each other by five
floors - which at six floors a day is roughly TWENTY HOURS apart, not a week.
There is NO fixed weekday schedule and no per-Titan reset day: the reset
instants drift (measured for one real cycle: Thu/Sat/Sun/Sun/Mon at times
between 01:04 and 20:04 UTC). Do NOT state a weekday for a tower reset. Ask the
`towers` tool for a live floor or timing - it is a verified port of the game's
own formula and is authoritative over this paragraph. (Both numbers here were
checked against it: advancing 35 days reproduces the current floors exactly
while advancing 7 days does not.) Entry
costs an Olympian Key; you earn Tower Shards, spent at the Titan Workshop on
Celestial weapon upgrades, Celestial classes and a personal tower. Added around
January 2023.

=== Flasks and Omniflask ===
Flasks are a Mage-tree battle mechanic (added 2025) with their own battle-menu
slot from tier 2. A flask charges as you cast elemental spells (faster when you
hit an enemy's weakness) and, in Towers and Monuments, its charge persists
between battles. Variants include Manaflask, Bloodflask, Ward Flask Infusion,
Mana-surge and Inflection; Omniflask spends a charged flask for an all-elements
blast. This is what "omniflask build" refers to.

=== Monuments and guild halls ===
Monuments (added late 2023) are single-player dungeons themed on Fallen Gods
(Ithra, Thor, Demeter, Vulcan) - free if you match their faction, otherwise a
Skeleton Key; they reward Proofs of Monument. The Titan Workshop gates Tower of
Olympia access and Titan weapons/classes. Kingdom guild halls add Guild Trials,
real-world "Settlements" (Duke -> Emperor titles), and live PvP.

=== Kingdoms (guilds) ===
A kingdom holds up to 50 members. You can JOIN one at level 25, but you must be
level 150 to CREATE one (founding costs about 100,000 orns). As of late 2025,
kingdoms are no longer faction-locked - members can be from different factions.
Kingdoms do wars, raid bosses and territory control.

=== Areas and territory ===
The world is divided into circular areas (~442m across; you interact within
~250m). Areas have guardians (a one-time double reward on first defeat) and, if
you control them, pay a small daily orn income. If you do not visit an area for
about 30 days you become a "ghost" there and your stats in it drop.

=== Followers and Bestial Bond ===
Followers (pets) are bought from Bestiaries and stored in your Keep (capacity
grows with Keep level); their stats scale to your level. Bestial Bond is the
tiered follower-synergy system: tier 1 makes the follower stronger and act more
often, tier 2 adds a chance to act twice per turn, tier 3 adds a chance to
protect you. It is strongest on the Valhallan/Beowulf line (Auriga lets
followers act twice per turn). The old "Beastmaster" is now the
Tamer -> Handler -> Spirit Tamer line. Note: a Summoner is NOT a follower class
- it auto-casts summon spells instead (Grand Summoner auto-casts two per
battle). Followers act on their own AI, not by direct command.

=== PvP: Arena and Colosseum ===
Arena and Colosseum fights are against an AI running another real player's
loadout, not a live opponent. Arena has Sparring/PvP/Mirror modes (one Arena
Token each). Colosseum needs your kingdom to have upgraded its Keep to a Castle,
costs 20 tokens, and is a fixed run of 20 fights whose opponents scale as you
win. No items are usable; HP/Mana always start full.

=== Currencies and materials ===
Gold buys and upgrades gear (endgame upgrades cost into the billions). Orns are
the premium currency: unlocking classes, swapping specs, buying followers,
building, and some upgrades. Proofs are per-guild materials used for high-tier
crafting. Other endgame currencies include Tower Shards and keys (Skeleton,
Olympian). Materials come from dismantling gear and are consumed by upgrades.

=== Stat bonuses (they multiply) ===
Several bonuses stack MULTIPLICATIVELY, not additively: Weapon Proficiency +5%
(Mage/Thief offensive stats), Origin Town +10% to core stats, and a Party bonus
+10% to core stats. Combined that is 1.05 × 1.10 × 1.10 ≈ +27%, not +25%.
