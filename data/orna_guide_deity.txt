* All Deities have the Second Chance and Steadfast 2 passives
* They also have a passive called Unstable Omnimancy that you can ignore.
* Only Base Deity can use the gear of Valhallan/Summoner classes, including the Celestial Lute.
* All deities have temporary buffs which can trigger at any time during a fight. Base’s temporary buff is Deific Channel (T. All +++) which doubles all stats. Ursa and Ara’s temp buffs are only attack or magic, but can stack up to a 3.75 damage multiplier.


Deity’s overall stats are very strong, except their Attack and Magic. Their damage can’t compete with the other classes out of the box, but they can become much stronger by building on their passive abilities: temporary buffs and red/bluelining.
Temporary buffs
DC has a 10% chance to trigger per turn, or per enemy you hit, meaning it occurs much more often in horde dungeons. Each of Ursa’s and Ara’s temp buff has a 2.5% chance.


You can’t make your passive temporary buffs trigger more often, but you can try to make them stay. Here are some pieces of equipment that can make positive status effects last longer.

Summary: each Deity has a strong native temporary buff. Therefore a good combo is to add more temporary buffs, and use the gear that will make them all last longer. Some good combos can come up.


	Base deity has native Deific Channel but no T. Att +++ or T. Mag +++. They’re the perfect target audience for Lugus Gauntlets.


	Using Lugus Gauntlet with Ursa or Ara is a bit redundant, but they go well with DC (either from a pet or self cast)


With a good setup, you can have a nice stack of buffs last a whole dungeon without spending turns casting them.
Redline/Blueline
Redlining and bluelining are mostly used in long raids or endless dungeons.


Redlining: none of the deity subclasses have High Tenacity, so you have to choose between zerk and redlining. Only Realmshifters can have both.
* Option 1: use zerk (1, 2 and/or 3) and find a way to regenerate enough HP to compensate.
* Option 2: cast Divine Bastion, then get your HP close to 1 with double/quad edge.
I use option 1 for short raids because it’s quicker to set up, and option 2 for longer raids (a.morri and AW).


Bluelining: deplete your mana, then try to keep a mana regen rate (with amities or adorns) equivalent to your spending. Deity’s Mana Siphon passive makes this strategy more random, but Ursa and Ara don’t have this problem.

Building up temporary buffs works well in horde dungeons, because the passives have a chance to trigger at every enemy you hit. If you have the gear to make positive status effects last longer, you can run entire dungeons without losing them.

	Dragon sweep is a popular build for melee classes to clear horde dungeons. The best weapon for it is a Celestial Lute filled with Hands of Selene. Other options are any high attack weapon, with a ruby to regen the hp you lose from zerk.
The main difference between Deity and other classes using the dragon sweep method, is that a deity should use a status effect amity (up to +50% buff duration) rather than a dragon damage amity (only up to +30% damage). And while you’re at it with temp buffs, you can throw in a couple Lugus gauntlets.
You can also use arcane sweep to run underworld portals faster, but you won’t have Bulwark’s dragon damage bonus.

Raids
Deity is not the best class to run raids quickly. Ideally, raids should be cleared quickly, which doesn’t give a lot of chances for your passive buffs to activate.
I typically have two loadouts for two types of raids:
Quick raids: zerk swash
“Quick raid” means you’re not waiting for Lugus Gauntlets to proc, and you’re not waiting for Lotan to cast his blights. Short buffing phase, then spam your attack skill. The whole thing should be done under 30s.

Towers
If you’re a fresh T10 and don’t want to spoil your account by playing GS or Beo, you can get started in towers by using one of the horde dungeon builds above. It will be slow because you’ll probably need to buff a couple turns at every fight.
Then once you have enough shards to unlock some celestial classes and weapons, you have at least two options to run towers faster.
Chained Shield
Unlock Gilgamesh Hercules to get Chained Shield, then build a celestial axe or lute.


CS damage is multiplied by your attack stat and ward. You need at least 200k ward to run through towers comfortably.
* Main hand: axe of Gilgamesh until you have a celestial axe or lute.
* Offhand: arisen shield until you have a spiked greatshield
* Head, chest and feet: whatever grants you the highest ward
* Amity: starting ward, ward regen, or damage boost.
CS consumes ward, so you need a way to regenerate it: amity, pet, and/or celestial augments in your weapon. Fill your celestial axe (or lute) with hands of Selene for damage, and feet of Prometheus for ward regen and find the right amount of each with some trial and error. I use 3 hands and 2 feet.
Ara Vesta
The other option, for heretics and magic enjoyers.
Anguish farming
The fastest way to farm proofs of anguish are boss horde dungeons, at the highest anguish level you can afford. This is pretty difficult so it takes some equipment and investment.


Ideally, you should unlock the Disciple (or Blade) of Finesse for its parting remark passive which grants extra turns when killing enemies. Then you can use the same Chained Shield build as for towers. The main difference is that you might need more ward regen to keep up during the entire dungeon.
PvP
Deity is arguably a strong PvP class. They won’t deal as much damage as glass cannon classes on the first turn, but they are tanky enough to survive and hit back when the passives hit.
* Spiked Shield (3 if unlocked). Same build as the CS build above, but more attack and less ward regen. For some reason, SS3 ignores the enemy’s dex so this is pretty OP.
* Swash crit build: glass cannon
* Turtle build: highest def/res you can, spam defensive buffs and divine bastion.
